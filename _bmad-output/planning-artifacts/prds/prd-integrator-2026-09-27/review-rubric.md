# PRD Quality Review — integrator

## Overall verdict
This is a well-built and unusually candid PRD: the Vision is specific to Nigerian money realities, the 47-entry Glossary is applied almost uniformly, all 68 FRs carry testable consequences (several with worked arithmetic), the seven journeys have named protagonists whose edge cases trace to FRs, and every counter-metric is paired with the metric it restrains. It is not yet green-light grade: 14 of 68 FRs (about 21%) rest on rules the PRD itself labels unconfirmed proposals or placeholders (cancellation, Credit Notes, Receipt rejection, loyalty numbers, the whole returns block), the Order and Payment lifecycle has holes where the money logic is densest, and three launch-relevant tensions are never raised as decisions: wholesale prices are visible at Level 2 to anyone with a NIN, nothing says a confirmed Payment must match funds actually received, and VAT-inclusive pricing has no tax-invoice requirement. There are no critical defects and five high-severity findings, all fixable inside the document.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are mostly stated as decisions, and the cost of the choice is often named. Open Question 6 in §8 (written OQ-6 from here on, and likewise OQ-n for the others) records "(Decided: one stock pool, one Store at launch...)"; FR-31's NOTE FOR PM calls overwriting Proposals "a deliberate v1 trade-off" and says what is lost (the platform "cannot reproduce what an earlier PDF quoted"); FR-64 admits the platform "passes on a shorter effective warranty"; and the §7 NOTE FOR PM says that at the first-assumed 20% growth "the model never reaches operating breakeven" and that inventory float "must be financed separately". Labelling proposals versus confirmed rules is rare and valuable. Nothing reads as "balanced against everything".

The candour is uneven, though. Of the seven NOTE FOR PM callouts, four sit at real tensions (FR-11 Manager and NIN, FR-31, the §6.2 wallet, §7), and three are routine hand-offs (FR-30 rounding, FR-66 wording, NFR-3 counsel). The tensions that most need a callout have none. An integrator asking "why can my client see wholesale prices?", or an investor asking "what stops a forged Receipt?", would find the objection dodged; one asking "why does my warranty start at dispatch?" or "why can't I recover an earlier Proposal?" finds it acknowledged.

The §8 list is honest but flat. It has no owner, date or blocked-FR mapping, and several items are already answered elsewhere or are listed three times (inline tag, §9 index, §8). The open-items density judgment is under Scope honesty.

### Findings
- **high** Level 2 price visibility contradicts the stated purpose of the Level 3 gate and is never defended (§2.2 Non-Users; §3 Access Level; FR-3; Constraints and Guardrails "Trust"; Addendum "Level 3 verification: alternatives considered") — §2.2 says non-integrators "are kept out of wholesale pricing by the Level 3 vouching gate" and the addendum says "the gate's job is mainly to keep wholesale prices from non-integrators", yet FR-3 and the Constraints ("Wholesale prices reach only Level 2 and above") make prices visible at Level 2, which needs only a NIN check and no trade verification. An end client, competitor or retail buyer can read the same wholesale Selling Prices that FR-30 takes great care to keep out of the Proposal PDF, and compare them with the Integrator's quote. For a product whose user earns an Integrator Margin on top of wholesale, that is an adoption risk, and the PRD neither names the trade-off nor says what was gained (an onboarding funnel, presumably). *Fix:* Decide explicitly in a NOTE FOR PM at FR-3. Either keep prices at Level 2 and rewrite §2.2 and the addendum to say the gate protects ordering, not price visibility (naming the exposure and why it is acceptable), or move price visibility to Level 3 and update FR-2, FR-3, UJ-2 and the Constraints.
- **high** "Payment by bank transfer confirmed by people" has no stated check against funds actually received, and receipt fraud is not on the Risks list (§1 Vision; §4.7 intro; UJ-6; FR-41; Risks and Mitigations; Addendum Level 3 rationale) — §4.7 says "Finance confirms each one against the Amount Due" and UJ-6 has Ngozi confirm "each uploaded Receipt", where a Receipt is "an uploaded photo or file". Nothing requires confirmation to match a credit on the platform's bank statement (amount, sender, reference). The addendum argues that "a fake buyer cannot cost the platform money by not paying" because Orders are prepaid, which ignores forged-receipt fraud, the obvious way a fake buyer takes goods without paying. Goods are released on Approval (FR-44) after this one human step. *Fix:* Define "confirm" in FR-41 as matching a bank-statement credit by amount, sender and an Order reference. Add receipt forgery and mismatched sender to Risks with a mitigation (reference narration, statement check before Paid, extra care on the first Orders of a new Level 3 account). Correct the addendum sentence.
- **medium** Unverified Managers hold powers that the single NOTE FOR PM does not cover (FR-5; FR-9; FR-10; FR-11 table and Notes; Glossary Business Account) — The FR-11 note questions only that "a Manager can spend the business's money". But FR-5 lets a Manager generate Vouch Codes (the addendum treats vouching as the platform's main trust control), FR-10 lets a Manager invite further Managers and "remove Users", and nothing stops a Manager removing the Owner although a Business Account "has exactly one Owner". FR-5 and FR-7 also never say the issuing Business Account and User are recorded and shown to the Admin reviewing a Level 3 request, so the vouching chain cannot be audited. *Fix:* Decide whether vouching and Manager invitations are Owner-only or need Owner approval. Add "a Manager cannot remove the Owner" to FR-10. Record the voucher on each code and show it in the FR-7 review. Extend the FR-11 note.
- **medium** A launch-critical rule is encoded as a testable consequence while the addendum says it will often be impractical (FR-68; UJ-7; §4.13 intro; OQ-14; Addendum "Support duties and the return window") — FR-68's "moves to Lapsed" after 48 hours is buildable as written. The addendum concedes "this will often be impractical" for Integrators far from the single launch Store, and that the clock also runs through a Manager's approval of a Technician-raised request. The decision is parked in OQ-14 with no default, so engineers will implement a rule its author expects to fail. *Fix:* Choose the default now (for example count a courier handover with a tracking reference as "returned", and pause the clock while a Technician-raised request awaits approval) and keep OQ-14 only for the zone-based extension.
- **low** Open Questions carry no owner, date or blocked-FR mapping, and several are answered or triple-listed (§8; §9; FR-11, FR-38, NFR-3) — OQ-8 (may a Technician see Order status) is answered by FR-11's table and FR-46. OQ-11 (Finance out of hours) is answered by FR-38's "Confirmation after the 24 hours does not remove a lock earned by an in-time upload". OQ-12 asks "who can see a stored NIN" though NFR-3 says "visible only to Admin". OQ-3, 4, 5, 7 and 10 restate inline tags that are also in §9, so each unconfirmed founder decision is declared three times and the few that truly block build (OQ-1, OQ-14, the second half of OQ-6) are diluted among 14. *Fix:* Split §8 into founder decisions (owner, default, needed-by, FRs blocked) and counsel or accountant confirmations. Fold answered items into FR text.
- **low** Drafting-process language leaks into an investor-facing document (§4.13 intro; FR-34, FR-41, FR-43, FR-57; §9) — "the assistant's proposal" appears six times, and §9 says "no objection yet; re-confirm at triage". An investor or architect cannot tell who the assistant is or what triage refers to. *Fix:* Replace with a per-FR status label (Confirmed, Proposed, Placeholder, Needs counsel) and a named owner for confirmation.

## Substance over theater — strong
The Vision fails the swap test in the right direction: it could not move into another PRD. It names Nigeria, dollar-tied prices, "a deliberate rule for what happens when the naira moves", "payment by bank transfer confirmed by people", markup-only revenue, and a falsifiable success criterion ("It succeeds if integrators come back to buy again"). There are six named people across seven journeys, which is above the four-persona threshold, but none is theater: Tunde (Owner), Hammed (prospect), Chidi (Technician) and Ngozi, Bola and Kunle map directly onto the roles in FR-2 to FR-7, FR-11 and FR-52 and drive permission and flow decisions. NFRs that exist carry thresholds (NFR-1 numbers, NFR-2 two hours, NFR-3 NIN encrypted, Admin-only and logged, NFR-4 stored rate, tier and VAT). Risks name a mechanism and the FRs that carry it. The addendum's breakeven model is real substance: the stated inputs reproduce (a USD 500 Order, USD 100 gross, about USD 80 contribution, about USD 1,800 fixed, so about 23 Orders a month, and month-11 breakeven at 60% growth). There is no differentiation section by design; market and competitive grounding is delegated to the brief.

### Findings
- **low** "Design" in the Vision outruns what the designer delivers (§1; FR-25, FR-26; §5 "No packaged 'solution' or bundle entity") — The designer is a room-organised bill of materials with live prices: no compatibility checks, quantity assistance or templates, and the Non-Goals exclude bundles. An investor reading "project-design platform" may expect more. *Fix:* Say "room-by-room bill of materials with live wholesale pricing" in §1, or name the design aids deferred to v2 in §6.2.
- **low** Small pockets of furniture (§2.1; NFR-5, NFR-6) — NFR-5 ("See FR-53") and NFR-6 ("See FR-28 and FR-50") are pointers that add numbering but no requirement. The loyalty job ("I want my loyalty to be recognised with better pricing") is feature-shaped rather than a job, and the last job switches to "As the platform team" story format. *Fix:* Fold the NFR stubs into FR-28, FR-50 and FR-53, and recast the loyalty job around the underlying need (a reason to consolidate buying on one supplier).

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is legible: one flow from first room to delivered equipment, built for Nigerian money realities, wins repeat purchases (§1). SM-1 (repeat-purchase rate) and SM-4 (Project-led share) test it, and SM-C1 to SM-C4 are each tied to the metric they restrain ("Counterbalances SM-1 and SM-3"), which is the strongest part of §7. The MVP is a complete transaction loop (access, design, proposal, order, payment, fulfilment, returns), a coherent revenue-led scope whose arithmetic is shown in the addendum.

What is at risk is that two of the mechanisms the thesis leans on work against it. The loyalty discounts push realized markup below the counter-metric's own floor, and the exchange-rate protection is thinner than the Vision implies. The metrics also do not instrument the core flow (Proposal to Order) or the second pillar (Documentation), and "deliberately narrow" is true of category and market but not of capability.

### Findings
- **medium** The Loyalty Tiers erode margin below SM-C1's own floor at the top tier, and SM-1 success moves accounts toward it (FR-21; FR-23 example; §7 SM-1, SM-6, SM-C1; Addendum "Breakeven model") — With a 25% Platform Markup, a Tier Discount comes straight out of margin. Tier 2 leaves a realized markup of 22.5%, Tier 3 exactly 20.0% (the SM-C1 floor, before any FX slippage), Tier 4 17.5%, and FR-23's own example (a 10% Back-order Discount) 12.5%. A Tier 4 discount alone is USD 30 on a typical USD 500 Order, 2.4 times the USD 12.50 the SM-6 model allows for all discounts and FX slippage, and takes contribution from about USD 80 to about USD 62.50. The PRD pairs SM-C1 with SM-1 and SM-3 but does not notice that the primary metrics move accounts up the tiers the counter-metric forbids. OQ-10 asks to "confirm the placeholder thresholds and discounts ... against real margins" without the arithmetic. *Fix:* Show the per-tier margin table in FR-21 or the addendum, then either cap discounts so every tier clears the SM-C1 floor (for example Tier 4 at 4% to 5%, and a ceiling on Back-order Discount) or restate SM-C1 as a blended target with an assumed tier mix.
- **medium** The exchange-rate protection is thinner than the Vision implies (§1; UJ-1 edge case; FR-29; FR-35; Glossary Proposal; Addendum "Breakeven model" FX line) — NGN 5 is about 0.4% of the NGN 1,330 rate the addendum uses, set against a one-month Proposal validity and a CBN-official versus parallel spread of NGN 46 to 61 in the same addendum (roughly ten times the band). UJ-1 presents "three weeks after sending the Proposal ... moved by more than NGN 5" as an edge case; on these numbers it is the normal case, so the Order will usually differ from the Proposal's cost-side total. No rationale is given for NGN 5 rather than a percentage, FR-20 never says which reference rate the Platform Rate follows, and USD pricing protects the platform only if Suppliers actually invoice in USD, which is not stated. *Fix:* State the rationale and expected hit rate, consider a percentage band or a time-boxed rate hold, name the reference rate in FR-20, and say whether Supplier Costs are USD invoices or converted naira quotes.
- **medium** The metrics do not instrument the core flow or the second pillar, and the first checkpoint is month 12 (§7; §1; §2.1; FR-48 to FR-50) — SM-4 counts Orders created from a Project, not Orders that follow a Proposal, so the funnel the Vision is built on (design, quote, order) has no conversion or time-to-quote measure. Installation Documentation is named in the Vision and has a journey (UJ-5) but no coverage target ("Documentation coming soon" is a legal default state for every Product), no author capacity in the three-role staff cost model, and no usage metric. With a ten-account launch cohort the first SM checkpoint is month 12, so the founder cannot learn whether the thesis holds for a year. *Fix:* Add Proposal-to-Order conversion within 30 days and time to first Proposal, Documentation coverage of ordered Products with a launch floor, and month-3 and month-6 cohort checkpoints (Level 3 activation, first Order, first Proposal, second Order).
- **medium** "Deliberately narrow" holds for category and market, not for capability, and the MVP has no cut line (§1; §6.1; FR-57 to FR-68; Addendum "Breakeven model" One-time build row) — §6.1 lists 13 capability areas and 68 FRs, including cancellation and an 11-FR returns and warranty block (FR-57 to FR-68, 12 FRs together) that the PRD labels unconfirmed and that no SM or Vision sentence connects to repeat purchase. The USD 30,000 build estimate cites "offline sync, multiple roles, and an audit trail" as its scope drivers and does not mention returns, Credit Notes, counterfeit quarantine, bulk upload, WhatsApp or Loyalty Tiers, so SM-6's month-47 recovery may rest on a cost that does not price this scope. Nothing says which FRs the first Order of the first ten Integrators needs versus fast-follow. *Fix:* Add a priority tier to §6.1 (first-Order critical, launch-week, fast-follow). Consider a manual-first v1 for returns (policy text, serial capture at dispatch, Credit Notes by Finance) that defers the Issue Code and Return Request state machine. Restate the build-cost assumption against the confirmed scope.
- **low** Several targets are one assumption stamped repeatedly, and some definitions cannot be computed as written (§7 SM-1, SM-2, SM-3, SM-5, SM-C1, SM-C3) — SM-2 (16, 26, 41, 66, 105 onboarded) and SM-3 (24, 38, 61, 98, 157) apply the same 60% a year to different counts, and SM-5 (800, 1,300, 2,000, 3,000, 4,500) follows a 50% to 60% curve, so none is independent evidence, and SM-5 (Catalogue breadth) is an activity count that validates nothing about the thesis. SM-1's population at month 12 is about 16 accounts, so one account moves the rate by six points. "Onboarded" (SM-2) is not a Glossary term. SM-C1 needs supplier payment timing that happens outside the platform. SM-C3 compares delivery to a Lead Time the Glossary defines as time "until a Back-order Item can ship", with no start event. *Fix:* Define SM terms against Glossary terms, show counts beside percentages, and name the data source for SM-C1 and the start event for SM-C3.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Per FR this is strong. Every one of the 68 FRs has a "Consequences (testable)" block, and many contain the real thing: FR-19's worked arithmetic (USD 125.00 plus 9.38 VAT is 134.38), FR-35's "NGN 5 or less keeps the Proposal Rate" boundary, FR-5's cap of two active codes, FR-38's hour-20 and hour-30 and USD 450 of USD 500 cases, FR-47's "exactly one Delivery Zone", and FR-68's "Support cannot generate an Issue Code for a serial number with no dispatch record". A few consequences only restate the FR (FR-48 "Supported formats are PDF, video, and written step-by-step guides"; FR-54 "opens without a browser address bar"; FR-51 "names the Order or Proposal it concerns").

The weakness is cross-FR. The Order and Payment lifecycle, the core of the product, has gaps in expiry, repricing, Unconfirmed Receipts and cancellation, and the price rules are internally inconsistent about currency. The NFR set is specific where it speaks and thin on availability, device support and retention. A downstream story writer will hit these first, so this dimension is held at adequate despite strong FR-level discipline.

### Findings
- **high** The Order and Payment lifecycle has four holes exactly where money rules are densest (FR-38; FR-41; FR-42; FR-51; FR-57) — (a) FR-38's assumption says the 48-hour expiry "is stopped by a Receipt upload", so any upload, even an Unconfirmed one, stops expiry and a partially paid Order never ends; FR-41's "until it expires" and "a transfer that Finance matches after the Order has expired" presuppose the opposite. (b) "Reprices at the current rate" does not say when the rate is sampled (hour 24, each confirmation, full payment), whether only the exchange rate moves or also Tier Discount and Supplier Cost, or how the Integrator learns the new Amount Due; FR-51's event list has no lock-deadline or repricing notice. (c) A Receipt uploaded in time but marked Unconfirmed (blurry photo, Finance error) and re-uploaded after hour 24 loses the lock ("uploaded within 24 hours ... once confirmed"), with no recourse because "An Admin override of repricing is deferred to v2", while the Risks list names the Finance bottleneck. (d) FR-57 says an Awaiting Payment Order "closes at no cost" and gives a Credit Note only to a Paid Order, so confirmed partial Payments on a cancelled Order are unaccounted for. *Fix:* Add an Order and Payment state table. Define expiry after the first Receipt (for example 48 hours from the last Receipt or Unconfirmed decision, with a hard maximum). Say repricing recomputes only the exchange rate, once, at a named moment, and notify it. Let a corrected re-upload within N hours of an Unconfirmed decision keep the lock. Credit confirmed partial Payments on cancel or expiry.
- **medium** Ordering a Project that contains an item that has become unorderable is unspecified (FR-34; FR-16; FR-26; FR-66; UJ-3 edge case) — FR-34 says an Order "contains every Project Item", FR-16 says an Out of Stock Product cannot be ordered unless back-order is on, and FR-66 leaves suspected-counterfeit items in Projects with a warning. UJ-3's edge case ("shows it as unavailable, or as a Back-order Item") is covered by no FR consequence even for Carts, and the Project analogue appears nowhere. A Project designed offline (FR-28) makes this likely. *Fix:* Add that at order creation unavailable Project Items are listed and the Owner or Manager removes them or orders the rest, amend FR-34's "every Project Item", and add the matching Cart consequence for UJ-3.
- **medium** Price currency and rounding are inconsistent between the Glossary, FR-19 and FR-38 (§3 Selling Price, Order; §4.3 intro; FR-19; FR-38; NFR-4) — The Glossary says Selling Price is "shown in naira at the Platform Rate", but FR-19's worked example yields "a Selling Price of USD 134.38" with VAT rounded in USD, while its rounding rule is "in the currency shown". At NGN 1,330 per USD, rounding in USD first gives NGN 178,725.40, against NGN 178,718.75 at full precision then rounded in naira, a NGN 6.65 difference the FR does not resolve. FR-38's examples ("A $450 Receipt on a $500 Order") are in dollars although Receipts evidence naira transfers. The currency of Amount Due, Payments, Credit Notes and Zone Rates is never stated, nor whether USD is ever shown to an Integrator. *Fix:* State each amount's currency and whether USD is displayed. Rewrite the FR-19 and FR-38 examples in NGN at a stated rate (for example at NGN 1,330: NGN 166,250.00 plus VAT NGN 12,468.75 is NGN 178,718.75) and apply the rounding rule to the displayed unit.
- **medium** Credit Note triggers and refund rules conflict across FR-43, the Glossary and §4.13 (§3 Credit Note; FR-43; FR-57; FR-61; FR-62; FR-66) — FR-43 lets Finance issue a Credit Note only "for an overpayment, or for an Order Line that cannot be fulfilled", while the Glossary lists four reasons and FR-57 (cancel), FR-61 (change of mind less 15%), FR-62 (freight) and FR-66 ("Finance credits affected Integrators") add more with no mechanism in FR-43. More importantly, FR-43 lets Owners and Managers "ask Finance ... to refund it by bank transfer", while FR-61 says "A cash refund by bank transfer is given only for a confirmed counterfeit or for non-delivery"; read together, an Integrator can convert a change-of-mind Credit Note into cash and defeat FR-61's restriction. *Fix:* List every Credit Note trigger once in FR-43 and state which Credit Note types are cash-refundable on request.
- **medium** Loyalty spend is not defined well enough to calculate, and the tier bands have gaps (FR-21; FR-5; §7 intro; Addendum "Loyalty tier history") — "Delivered spend ... converted to USD equivalent" does not say whether it is Wholesale Price after discount or the VAT-inclusive Amount Due, whether delivery counts, which rate converts naira (the Order's stored rate or the current one), or whether returns and Credit Notes reduce spend (only cancellations are excluded). The "typical USD 500 Order" is "before VAT" in §7 and "at Wholesale Price" in the addendum. The bands "1,500 to 4,999" and "5,000 to 11,999" leave 4,999.01 to 4,999.99 in no tier. "3 Delivered Orders" for Established Integrator has no minimum value and no netting of returns. *Fix:* Define spend (ex-VAT, goods only, after discount, at the Order's stored rate, net of returns) and write bands as "at least 1,500 and under 5,000".
- **medium** The returns flow is specified only for a faulty device, and several terms are orphaned (FR-58; FR-60; FR-61; FR-62; FR-66; FR-68; Glossary Return Request; UJ-7) — FR-68 gates every Return Request on an Issue Code that Support generates "if remote assistance does not fix the fault", but FR-58 also admits change of mind, wrong item, transit damage and counterfeit, where there is no fault to fix remotely. FR-61 allows a cash refund "for non-delivery", yet a Return Request is "about a delivered Order Line" and no FR defines a non-delivery claim (a lost courier parcel, a Shipment stuck In-transit). FR-66 acts on a "Product or batch" but no FR or Glossary term defines batch or lot tracking. FR-62 reimburses "reasonable freight" with no cap. FR-60 has "Decided" and "Resolved" with no distinction and "Rejected" with no actor. At launch Operations holds both Support investigation and the decision, with no separation of duties. *Fix:* Branch FR-68 by reason. Define or drop non-delivery and batch. Give freight a cap or a Zone Rate equivalent. Define Decided versus Resolved and who can Reject.
- **medium** The Finance confirmation SLA is stated four ways and has no mechanism (NFR-2; FR-41; SM-C4; UJ-6; FR-4; FR-6; FR-51) — NFR-2 says Finance "confirms uploaded Payments within two hours during Business Hours", FR-41 says "aims to", SM-C4 targets 95% "within two Business Hours", and UJ-6 says "Every Receipt uploaded in the morning is confirmed within two hours". No FR gives Finance a work queue, an age indicator or an alert, although FR-4 and FR-6 give Admin queues, and nobody is notified when a Receipt is uploaded (FR-51 notifies Integrators only). *Fix:* Pick one commitment and use it in all four places. Add a Finance queue FR (oldest first, age indicator, alert at a stated threshold). State how "two Business Hours" counts across 6pm and Sunday.
- **medium** The Platform Rate, the one input that reprices the whole Catalogue, has a vague trigger and no guardrail (FR-20; Glossary Platform Rate; Addendum "Breakeven model" FX note) — "changing it only when it has moved meaningfully" is not testable, a change "takes effect immediately on all displayed prices", and the only control is an audit entry. A typing slip (NGN 133 for 1,330) reprices every Product at once. The PRD does not name the reference the rate follows, although the addendum cites a CBN official rate of 1,329 and a parallel rate of 1,375 to 1,390. *Fix:* Define the trigger (for example a change of more than X% since last set) or drop the phrase. Add a consequence that a change above Y% needs a second confirmation showing a sample price. Name the reference basis.
- **medium** NFR coverage is specific where it speaks and silent on several launch-grade qualities (§4 Cross-Cutting NFRs; FR-54; FR-55; Addendum "Delivery surface") — There is no availability or recovery target for a platform that holds Orders and Payments (no uptime in Business Hours, backup, recovery point or time), no supported device and browser floor for an installable offline app (iOS Safari handles install, storage quota and background sync differently from Android Chrome), no retention rules beyond the open NIN question (Receipts, client details on Proposals, delivery addresses, audit log), and performance is bounded only for the Catalogue list ("about 5 seconds on a 3G-class connection"). *Fix:* Add NFRs for availability and recovery, the supported device and browser floor with the offline behaviour expected on each, retention (with counsel), and a performance budget for Project open, Proposal generation and Receipt upload.
- **medium** Several capabilities are referenced by one FR but owned by none (FR-1; FR-3; FR-6; FR-8; FR-11; FR-30; FR-33; FR-63; Glossary VAT) — "The terms accepted at sign-up state these exclusions" (FR-63; Constraints and Guardrails "Terms") but FR-1 has no acceptance or versioning of terms. FR-30 prints the business "name and logo" but no FR captures a logo (FR-6 collects only a "trade name", and UJ-2 and FR-30 say "business name"). The Glossary says VAT "is a setting that only Admin can change", but FR-52 and FR-53 do not list it. "Change Owner" appears only in FR-11's table, with no process and no NIN step for the new Owner. FR-8 does not say whether Admin-seeded Owners are NIN-verified, though NFR-3 and the FR-11 note treat the Owner's NIN as the identity record. FR-33 does not say whether a Cart belongs to a User or a Business Account. *Fix:* Add the missing FRs or consequences, one line each.
- **low** Vague terms remain in otherwise testable text (FR-20 "meaningfully"; FR-32 "clearly marked"; FR-41 "a clear message"; FR-62 "reasonable freight"; NFR-1 "comfortably"; FR-29; FR-30) — FR-30's consequence "No cost-side figure can be derived from a text search of the PDF" does not say what is searched (Supplier Cost, Wholesale Price, Platform Markup, Tier Discount, Integrator Margin, or PDF metadata). FR-29 sets no bounds on Integrator Margin (zero, negative, maximum) or on which client details are required. *Fix:* Replace with a forbidden-field list tested against visible text and metadata, give messages literal text or a UX string reference, and add Margin bounds.
- **low** Offline behaviour has untestable edges (FR-27; FR-28; FR-50; UJ-5) — FR-28 keeps "the earlier version ... for recovery" with no consequence or recovery action. FR-50 makes offline Documentation a manual action ("A User can save"), while UJ-5's entry state assumes the Project and its Documentation "were synced earlier", so no FR guarantees what UJ-5 needs. There are no storage-limit or eviction rules, and FR-27 shows prices offline with no staleness marker even though the NFR bullet says offline prices are never used for a Proposal or Order. *Fix:* Add a "save this Project for offline" action or automatic caching of a Project's Documentation, a recovery consequence, and an "as of" marker on offline prices.
- **low** Notifications are specified as an event list, not as behaviour (FR-51; Addendum "Integrations to plan for") — The one consequence ("names the Order or Proposal it concerns") says nothing about channel choice, failure and retry, WhatsApp opt-in, or the money-relevant events missing from the assumed list (lock deadline, repricing, Credit Note issued, Order cancelled). "Order status changes" could mean a WhatsApp message per Shipment status, which the addendum says to avoid. *Fix:* Freeze the launch event list by ID, add opt-in capture and an email fallback on failure, and add the missing money events.
- **low** The Back-order opt-in does not disclose its irreversibility (FR-36; FR-57; FR-63; UJ-4) — The opt-in shows "Back-order Discount and Lead Time", but FR-57 says a Back-order Item already ordered from a Supplier "cannot be cancelled" and FR-63 makes Back-order Items non-returnable for change of mind. *Fix:* Require both statements on the opt-in screen and add them to FR-36's consequences.
- **low** The warranty expiry shown may exceed the manufacturer's actual cover (FR-64; Glossary Warranty Period) — The clock runs "from the date the platform dispatches the goods", but the FR-64 assumption's own reason is that manufacturers start theirs "at distributor shipment", which is earlier for stock that sat in the warehouse. The displayed expiry can therefore be later than the manufacturer will honour, while the platform offers "no platform warranty beyond dead on arrival". "Passes on a shorter effective warranty" describes the Integrator's remaining cover, not the date shown. *Fix:* State who bears the gap, and show the earlier of the dispatch-based and manufacturer-based dates where the manufacturer date is known.

## Scope honesty — adequate
Omissions are mostly explicit and de-scoping is honest rather than silent. §5 lists seven Non-Goals, §6.2 lists eight deferrals, most with a target phase, FR-38 carries "Out of Scope: An Admin override of repricing is deferred to v2", FR-45 says "v1 has no automated courier integration", FR-68 puts remote assistance "outside the platform", and FR-66 puts regulator reports "outside the platform". `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are specific, labelled by provenance, and indexed. The gaps are three omissions that are only half-visible (tax invoice, Credit Notes versus "no stored value", supplier-side procurement) and one tagging lapse.

Open-items density, counted: 33 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (30 of 68 FRs carry at least one, plus UJ-7, the Glossary and §7's blanket tag), 7 NOTE FOR PM, 14 Open Questions, and a 28-entry index. Fourteen FRs (21%) are labelled unconfirmed proposals or placeholders. Many tags are harmless defaults (FR-15, FR-16, FR-50, FR-55) that engineers can build on. The ones that matter are the money and legal exceptions. Judgment: the labelling is exemplary, but the density on the consequential items is too high for an unqualified green light. It is acceptable only as a staged green light.

### Findings
- **high** Open-items density is a build blocker for about a fifth of the FR set (§4.13 intro; FR-21, FR-34, FR-41, FR-43, FR-57, FR-59 to FR-64, FR-66 to FR-68; §8; §9) — The unconfirmed set is the exception handling (cancellation, Credit Notes, Receipt rejection, repeat Orders), the loyalty numbers and the returns and warranty block; the happy path (FR-33, FR-35 to FR-40, FR-42, FR-44 to FR-47) is largely founder-confirmed. These rules drive the data model and state machines and carry legal exposure (the 12-month Credit Note expiry, the 15% restocking fee, warranty wording that "counsel should vet"), so building from them before sign-off risks rework and exposure. *Fix:* Add a status per FR (Confirmed, Proposed, Placeholder, Needs counsel) and a build baseline. Confirmed FRs and low-consequence defaults proceed to epics; Proposed money and legal FRs wait for founder and counsel sign-off, or ship manual-first.
- **high** There is no tax-invoice requirement although every price includes VAT and the addendum says to plan as VAT-registered (§8 OQ-1; FR-19; FR-30; Addendum "Compliance notes") — OQ-1 treats "whether the platform issues tax invoices" as open, and no FR produces any invoice, order confirmation or payment receipt document for an Integrator. A VAT-registered seller charging VAT-inclusive prices to business buyers will in all likelihood have to issue invoices, and buyers who reclaim input VAT will ask for them (to be confirmed with the accountant, as OQ-1 already says). The addendum also reports e-invoicing starting July 2027 for businesses under NGN 1bn. *Fix:* Add an FR for a tax invoice (or equivalent document) on each Paid Order and each Credit Note, with fields settled with the accountant. Reduce OQ-1 to "what fields and when". List e-invoicing readiness as an architecture constraint.
- **medium** The Non-Goals and Constraints say "no stored value" while Credit Notes behave like a held balance (§5 "Not a payments company: no wallet, no stored value"; Constraints and Guardrails "no spendable on-platform balance"; FR-43; Addendum "Wallet research") — FR-43 lets Finance apply a Credit Note "against a later Order's Amount Due" and lets it sit for 12 months, and the addendum's own counsel question is "whether an advance-credit ledger counts as deposit-taking". FR-43's assumption admits the expiry and refund terms "need counsel's view", but the Non-Goal reads as unqualified, and an investor or counsel will notice the tension. *Fix:* Reword to "no top-up wallet; the only balance held is a Credit Note arising from an overpayment, stock-out, cancellation or approved return", and put the Credit Note ledger on the counsel list with the wallet questions.
- **medium** Supplier-side procurement and receiving are silently out of scope although two rules depend on them (FR-12; FR-15; FR-57; FR-66; SM-C3; Glossary Back-order Item) — FR-57 says a Back-order Item "the platform has already ordered from a Supplier cannot be cancelled", and SM-C3 measures lateness against Lead Time, but no FR records that a Supplier order was placed or that goods were received; stock is edited by hand (FR-12) or bulk upload (FR-15). Supplier notification and chargeback for counterfeit stock ("Operations notifies the Supplier") are likewise off-platform. None of this is a Non-Goal. *Fix:* Add a Non-Goal ("no purchase-order, inbound receiving or supplier chargeback workflow in v1") and, if FR-57 and SM-C3 stay, a minimal "ordered from Supplier" and "received" marker on back-order Order Lines.
- **low** Two proposals are not tagged where a reader meets them (FR-63; FR-66; FR-67; §9) — §9 lists FR-63, FR-66 and FR-67 inside grouped entries, but none carries an inline `[ASSUMPTION]`; their proposal status is visible only in the §4.13 intro, OQ-2 and the index. A reader of FR-63 alone sees settled policy ("These are not returnable for change of mind"). *Fix:* Tag them inline or give FR-63 and FR-66 the status label proposed above.

## Downstream usability — strong
This is a chain-top PRD, and it is built to be sourced from. The 47-entry Glossary opens with "Downstream workflows and readers must use these terms exactly". FR, UJ, SM, NFR and OQ IDs are contiguous and unique, and every cross-reference resolves (details below). Each FR carries its own "Realizes UJ-n" and a consequences block, so epics can map FR to story directly, UX can lift the journeys as written, and architecture gets the addendum's "Delivery surface", "Integrations to plan for" and "Fulfilment notes". The weaknesses are concentrated in cross-cutting definitions (restricted data, permission matrices, the Product attribute list, the status model) that downstream teams will have to assemble from several FRs.

### Findings
- **medium** The restricted-data rule is stated six ways and the permission tables are incomplete (FR-2; FR-11 table and consequence; FR-19; FR-27; NFR-3; Constraints and Guardrails "Pricing"; FR-52) — The Technician and Level 1 data restriction, the most security-critical rule in the PRD, is listed differently each time. FR-11's consequence names "Wholesale Price, Tier Discount, Integrator Margin, Payment, or Amount Due" (not Selling Price, Supplier Cost, Platform Markup, Credit Notes or Proposal totals), FR-2 says "price, discount, or markup", FR-19 says "these values", and NFR-3 says "Cost, markup, margin, and Payment data". A QA writer cannot derive one test matrix. FR-11 omits capabilities granted elsewhere (generate Vouch Codes, request Level 3, see and request Credit Notes, cancel an Order, use a Cart), and FR-52 omits changing the VAT rate (Glossary), editing reporting windows (FR-59), marking a counterfeit (FR-66) and creating Level 3 accounts directly (FR-8), although FR-52 promises "A Staff member can use only the capabilities their role holds". *Fix:* Add one "Restricted data" definition with per-role visibility, referenced by every FR above, and complete both tables.
- **medium** The Product and Order Line data model must be assembled from six FRs (FR-12; FR-59; FR-63; FR-64; FR-65; FR-66; Glossary Product) — FR-12's field list ("name, description, images, Product Range, Supplier Cost, stock quantity, Stock Threshold, Lead Time, back-order setting, and Documentation") omits attributes later FRs need: a serialised flag (FR-65), Warranty Period (FR-64), a dead-on-arrival window (FR-59 "where the Product records one"), a returnability class (FR-63's end-of-life, refurbished, demonstration, software and licence, and "special orders", a term that exists nowhere else), a counterfeit status (FR-66), and brand, model or SKU. Architecture will discover these piecemeal. *Fix:* Add a Product attribute list, and an Order Line list (serial numbers, warranty expiry, back-order flag, price components), to FR-12 or the Glossary.
- **low** The status model is scattered, and the addendum's copy is stale (FR-38; FR-42; FR-44; FR-45; FR-60; Glossary Delivered Order; Addendum "Status naming") — Order statuses appear only in FR-45's assumption (Awaiting Payment, Paid, Approved, Completed, Expired, Cancelled), and the addendum omits Cancelled. "Completed" (FR-45) and "Delivered Order" (Glossary, FR-5, FR-21, SM-1 to SM-3) are the same state under two names, and "Completed" is not in the Glossary. Payment outcome, Shipment, Return Request, Proposal and code statuses live in separate FRs. *Fix:* Add one status table (entity, statuses, transitions, trigger, actor) in §4.8 or the Glossary, pick one name for the delivered state, and fix the addendum.

## Shape fit — strong
A multi-stakeholder B2B product with meaningful UX is exactly where named-protagonist journeys are load-bearing, and the PRD delivers them. The seven UJs share one structure (persona and context, entry state, path, climax, resolution, edge case), each has a named protagonist with context inline, and the edge cases that matter trace to FRs: the rate move in UJ-1 to FR-35, the failed NIN check in UJ-2 to FR-4, the sold-out item in UJ-4 to FR-18 and FR-43, the unknown serial number in UJ-7 to FR-67 and FR-68. The offline need in UJ-5 is covered by FR-28 and FR-50. The PRD is not over-formalized for the stakes and not under-formalized overall. The thin spots are the staff side, the Manager, and the voucher.

### Findings
- **medium** UJ-6 is a duty list, not a journey, and the staff-side flows the product's operations depend on have no journey with decision points (UJ-6; FR-41; FR-43; FR-52) — UJ-6's path lists what three named staff do in a day. Its Climax ("Every Receipt uploaded in the morning is confirmed within two hours") is an SLA rather than a moment, and its Resolution ("No Order is waiting on a person outside Business Hours") is a negation. The manual Receipt check, the most operationally critical step in the product, has no flow for UX: how Ngozi finds the Receipt, what she checks it against, what the Unconfirmed message says, and when she raises a Credit Note. *Fix:* Give Ngozi her own journey for one Receipt (queue, bank check, Unconfirmed branch, Credit Note) and leave the rest as the role matrix in FR-52.
- **medium** Two decision-bearing roles have no named protagonist (UJ-2; UJ-7; FR-5; FR-10; FR-58) — The Manager approves Technician-raised returns (UJ-7 "Tunde's Manager") and can generate Vouch Codes, invite, order and pay, but is never a protagonist. The voucher in UJ-2 ("his voucher generates a new one") is both the growth loop and the abuse vector, and is also unnamed. "Support" in UJ-7 is unnamed too. UX will need all three. *Fix:* Name the Manager, the voucher and the Support officer, add the Manager's half of UJ-1 or UJ-3 (placing and paying for an Order) and the voucher's half of UJ-2.
- **low** UJ-7 covers only the faulty-device path (UJ-7; FR-57; FR-58; FR-59; FR-61) — Cancellation with a Credit Note, a change-of-mind return with the 15% fee, transit damage and a counterfeit report have no journey or edge case. *Fix:* Add one short edge case each for cancellation and change of mind to UJ-1 or UJ-3.

## Mechanical notes
**Glossary drift**
- **Wholesale Price versus Selling Price.** The Glossary defines both cleanly (Wholesale Price: USD, before discount and VAT; Selling Price: after Tier or Back-order Discount, plus VAT, shown in naira). Drift: FR-11's visibility row and its Technician test use "Wholesale Price" for what Owners and Managers see, while FR-3 says Users see "Selling Prices". FR-27 says "line prices, Room totals, and Project totals" with no term. FR-30 says "client prices". FR-35 says "the Proposal's cost-side total" and "the Order's total"; if "Order's total" means the Amount Due it includes delivery and the Proposal total does not, so the difference shown mixes bases. FR-19's example calls USD 134.38 a Selling Price although the Glossary says naira. Which of the two an Integrator sees in the Catalogue, in a Project and on an Order is never stated.
- **Manager (Integrator role) versus staff "manager".** Clean. Capitalised Manager is used only for the Integrator-side role (FR-5, FR-10, FR-11, FR-58, UJ-7). The only lowercase "manager" is the founder's quoted phrase in FR-56's assumption and its §9 line, which says "the platform has no staff role called manager", the right handling. §4.13's intro uses "Integrator Manager", an unglossed qualifier that is harmless but unnecessary.
- **Support versus Operations.** Consistent through the Glossary ("Operations holds these duties at launch"), FR-52's "Support duties" row, §6.1 and the addendum. However, FRs and UJ-7 write "Support can look up ..." as if a role existed (risking an extra role in the data model that the PRD says should not exist at launch), UJ-6 omits Support duties from Bola's day, and FR-66 lets "Support" flag a counterfeit although FR-52's Support row does not list that capability. Prefer "staff holding Support duties" in FRs.
- **Delivered Order versus Completed.** The same state under two names (FR-45 versus the Glossary and §7); "Completed" is not in the Glossary.
- **business name versus trade name.** UJ-2 and FR-30 say "business name", FR-6 and its §9 line say "trade name", FR-3's profile has neither.
- **Store versus collection point.** UJ-7, FR-56 and FR-62 use "collection point" for a non-Main Store; it is not a Glossary term.
- **account.** Used informally for Business Account (UJ-2, FR-1, FR-4, FR-9) and, in FR-1, for the login. Access Level has no stated subject: FR-2 says "Level 1 Users", FR-3 and FR-7 say the Business Account holds the Level.
- **Order versus Order Line.** UJ-7 has the Manager match "the specific Order"; FR-58 says "Order Line".
- **Level 3 request versus "Vouch Code requests".** UJ-6 says Kunle reviews "Vouch Code requests"; FR-6 and FR-7 say "Level 3 request".
- **onboarded** (SM-2), **cost-side total** (FR-35) and **client prices** (FR-30) are not Glossary terms. **NIN**, **RMA**, **SON** and **FCCPC** are never expanded, which matters for an investor reader.
