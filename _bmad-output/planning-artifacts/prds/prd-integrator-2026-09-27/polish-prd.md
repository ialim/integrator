## Purpose and audience read

This document exists to help the founder, investors, UX designers, architects, and story writers agree on one buildable set of requirements (80 FRs, 7 user journeys) for a B2B wholesale and project-design platform that systems integrators in Nigeria use to design, quote, and order equipment. Correct me if the premise is wrong; every row below rests on it.

## Structure model

**Strategic/Context (Pyramid)**, the model for PRDs. The document also follows the Reference model inside §3 (Glossary) and §4 (one schema per FR: Status line, body, Consequences, Notes), which was checked for consistency.

Fit against the model:

- **Top-down:** partly met. §0 and §1 orient well, but the status, the open items, and the build order are only in §6 and §8 and are never summarised up front (S1, S11, S13).
- **Grouping:** mostly met. The FR schema and the user-journey schema are consistent. Three blocks are unnumbered, and two of them sit mid-document (S5). State lists are scattered (S6).
- **Ordering:** the pricing and payment core is in the right place, but the heaviest rules are single paragraphs (S2, S8), and FR-65 sits in the wrong section (S9).
- **MECE:** §5 and §6.2 overlap (S4), FR-80 and NFR-4 list the same stored record twice (S10), and Constraints recaps FR text (S12).
- **Evidence:** met. §7 leads with targets; the benchmarks sit in the addendum.

Real redundancy is low. Only 10 of the 203 Consequences bullets restate their FR body almost word for word, and no passage of more than 25 words is copied between sections. No row is tagged CUT.

## Voice and style notes (preserved, not edited)

- Requirement-style prose: terse, semicolon-heavy, italic status lines, "Consequences (testable)" bullets, `[ASSUMPTION]` and `[NOTE FOR PM]` flags, spaced em dashes as Glossary separators, serial commas, sentence-case headings.
- British spelling in the body (authorisation, normalised, serialised, licence, fulfilment, cancelled). It suits the Nigerian audience, so it is kept even though the Microsoft guide defaults to US spelling. The American outliers are in the closing line.
- Business time counted in words ("two Business Hours", "one Business Day"), calendar spans in numerals ("48 hours", "7 days").
- The Vision's lowercase "integrator" for the profession and the product name, and the first-person voice of the Jobs To Be Done, are deliberate and left alone.
