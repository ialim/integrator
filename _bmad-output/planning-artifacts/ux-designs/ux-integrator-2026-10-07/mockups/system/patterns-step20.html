Step 20 of the walk-through

Patterns: components, states and interaction rules

Proposal for your reaction. Across the 19 steps you have confirmed, the same small set of parts keeps coming back. This sheet names them once, shows each as it has been drawn, and says how it behaves, so the build and the two spines describe them the same way. It then lists the states every screen must have, and the interaction rules for keyboard, touch, time and low bandwidth. Nothing here adds a new look. It describes what is already on the screens, with a few rules written down for the first time. Nothing is decided until you confirm it.

A. Components

Sixteen parts. Looks are in the design system (DESIGN.md). This is how each one behaves.

1. Buttons

Send receiptSave draftDecline…Approve
  • One filled button per screen. It is the main thing to do.
  • At least 44 px high, 52 px in a phone form. Icon-only buttons are only the cart and Back, and they have a name.
  • A button that cannot be pressed stays visible and dashed, and the reason is written beside it.
  • While it works, it says "Sending…" and cannot be pressed twice.

  • The words always carry the meaning. Colour only reinforces it.
  • One label, one meaning, the same word on every screen.

C. Interaction rules

The rules for how people operate the product, across a phone, a keyboard and a poor connection.

RuleWhat it says
TargetsAt least 44 px for anything pressed on a phone, with space between. 24 px is the WCAG 2.2 minimum and is never the goal. Rows with a checkbox are 48 px.
FocusA 3 px green ring with a 2 px gap on everything that can be reached, never removed. The order follows the reading order. A skip link jumps past the menu. Sheets and dialogs hold the focus, and give it back.
KeyboardEverything works without a mouse. Esc closes a sheet or dialog. Enter acts on the focused main button. In the console, J, K, Enter and / move around lists. Money actions are never a single key.
GesturesNothing needs a swipe, a long press, a pinch or a drag. Reordering uses arrows. Pull to refresh also has a Refresh button.
When to ask "Are you sure?"Before something that cannot be undone (closing an account, a hold, revoking approval), before money moves (confirm payment, a refund, a reversal), and before something is sent to someone else (an invitation, a shared code). Not for edits that can be undone.
UndoA deleted room can be brought back for 30 days (Step 2). A saved change can be restored from an earlier version for 30 days. A confirmation is not a substitute for undo where undo is possible.
Keeping what was typedAfter any failure, the form keeps its values. A project saves itself, and works offline. A receipt upload resumes. A long console form saves a draft.
TimeoutsNothing times out without saying so. A business is signed out after 30 idle days. The console warns 5 minutes before its 12 hours end, with a Stay signed in button. A code shows the time it has left.
Proving it is youNo puzzle that tests memory or reading. Codes can be pasted. Passkeys are offered. Bot checks are invisible.
MotionNothing moves unless it helps. No autoplay. Changes are at most 150 ms. A bar does not animate. The system's reduce-motion setting is always obeyed.
FeedbackA pressed button changes at once. The result is said within one second, or a bar shows progress. Success and failure are always in words.
InstallingThe app is offered for the home screen after the second visit, or right after the first "Save for offline". It is always in Account. On an iPhone the steps are written out in words.
Weak dataText first, pictures on request, small thumbnails, no autoplay video, lists in pages of 20, receipts resized on the phone to about 2 MB. A Refresh button never needs a gesture.
Camera and filesPermission is asked for only when the person presses "Take a photo". If it is refused, "Choose a file" is offered and the screen says so.
