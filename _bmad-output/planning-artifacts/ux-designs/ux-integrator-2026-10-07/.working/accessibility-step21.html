Step 21 of the walk-through

The accessibility floor

Proposal for your reaction. You said this product is regulated, and that it must meet WCAG 2.2 AA, so this is not a nice-to-have. This sheet sets the floor in five parts: the standard we hold ourselves to, the colour evidence worked out from the actual tokens, the rules by area, the four small changes it makes to screens you have already confirmed, and how we prove it before launch. The look of contrast belongs to the design system (DESIGN.md). The behaviour belongs to EXPERIENCE.md. Nothing here is decided until you confirm it.

1. The standard

WCAG 2.2, level AA, on every screen

The app on a phone and on a desktop browser, the staff console, and the PDFs, emails and WhatsApp messages it produces. Level A and AA are met in full. Where AAA is cheap (a 3 px focus ring, plain language), we take it.

English, light, one language

The page language is set to English for Nigeria. No other language and no dark mode in version 1, as you decided, so there is nothing to translate and nothing to invert. Reading level is aimed at about Grade 8 and checked when copy is reviewed.

3. The rules, by area

AreaWhat we do
Text size and zoomBody 15 px, inputs 16 px so a phone never zooms in by itself, nothing under 12 px. Everything works at 200% with no loss. The phone app reflows to a 320 px width with no sideways scrolling. The system's larger-text setting is honoured.
Screen readersEvery screen has the same landmarks (header, navigation, main) and one top heading, then headings in order. Lists, tables with captions and column headings, and timelines as ordered lists. Icon-only buttons have a name ("Cart, 2 items"). A progress bar has its figure in words. A switch is announced as a switch with its word. Code boxes are one labelled group.
AnnouncementsInformation is announced politely, failures at once. Going offline and back online is announced. A countdown is announced at the start and at 1 hour, 15 minutes and the end, never every tick.
ImagesA product photo is named for the product. A decorative image says nothing. A photo in a guide has a short description, written by the person who adds it, so a technician who cannot see it still gets the step.
FormsA visible label for every field. Errors are in words beside the field and are also listed at the top of a long form, with a suggestion. Anything legal or financial can be checked, corrected or undone before it is final. Nothing is asked twice in the same journey: the sender's details are remembered, the proposal's client details fill the order, the voucher's details fill the request.
Signing inNo test of memory or reading. Codes can be pasted and filled in. Passkeys are offered.
Getting help"Call or WhatsApp us" is always in the same place: the last block of a screen, and under Account.
Movement and pointersNo drag, no shake, no tilt. An action happens on release, so it can be cancelled by moving away. A pointer-only gesture always has a plain button as well.
Focus is never hiddenA sticky bar (the tab bar, a "Done" bar, a console header) never covers what has focus. The page scrolls the focused control clear of it.
TimeNothing times out unseen. The console warns before it ends a session. A code shows its time left. Offers that matter, like a price hold, say their exact end time.
Motion and flashingNothing flashes. Nothing moves unless it helps. The system's reduce-motion setting is obeyed.
Windows high contrastBorders are real borders, not shadows, so a control is still visible when the system forces its own colours.
Documents we produceProposals, parts lists, tax invoices and credit-note documents are tagged PDFs: real text, a title, the language, headings and true tables, and a description for the logo. WhatsApp and email are plain text first, with links that say where they go.

4. What this changes in screens you have confirmed

Small text goes up to 12 px

The small capital labels above headings and a few notes were drawn at 11 px. They become 12 px. Nothing else changes in the layout.

The staff console reflows when zoomed

It is built for a desktop, 1280 px wide, as decided. Below 1024 px, which includes anyone who zooms in, the rail becomes a menu button and side-by-side panes stack. A wide table scrolls inside its own box. Without this, a staff member who zooms to 400% could not use it.

A white ring on the green bars

On the green top bar and on filled green buttons, focus shows as a white ring, because a green ring would vanish.

A photo description in the guide editor

Step 18's guide editor gets one more field under each photo: "Describe the photo, for people who cannot see it". A step with a photo and no description cannot be published.

5. How it is proved

WhenWhat is checked
On every changeAn automated accessibility check runs on every screen and every state in the pattern sheet. A new failure stops the release.
In every storyThe acceptance criteria say what a keyboard user, a screen-reader user and someone zoomed to 200% can do. A story is not done until they can.
Before each releaseA person goes through the main journeys using only a keyboard, then with a screen reader (TalkBack and VoiceOver for the app, NVDA for the console), then zoomed to 200% and 400%, then with reduced motion and forced colours.
Before launchReal users test it, including people who use a screen reader and people with low vision, on a phone, on weak data. What they find is fixed or written down and decided.
After launchA published accessibility statement with the "Call or WhatsApp us" line. Every report is a case, like any other.
